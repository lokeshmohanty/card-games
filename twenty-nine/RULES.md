# 29

The traditional Indian trick-taking game. Four players in two partnerships: seats 1 and 3 play against seats 2 and 4, partners sitting opposite. Bots fill empty seats.

## The cards
- 32 cards: 7, 8, 9, 10, J, Q, K and A in four suits.
- In every suit they rank, high to low: **J 9 A 10 K Q 8 7**.
- Four cards carry points: **J 3, 9 2, A 1, 10 1**. That is 7 a suit, 28 in the pack. The others are worth nothing.

## A hand
- **The deal.** Everyone gets four cards.
- **The bidding.** Starting after the dealer, each player bids or passes. A bid is the points your side promises to take, from **16 to 28**, and must beat the last bid. Once you pass you are out of the bidding. When only one bidder is left, they win the bid. If the first three pass, the fourth must bid 16.
- **The trump.** The bidder picks the trump suit in secret by laying one of their cards aside face down. Only they know it. Then everyone gets four more cards.
- **The play.** The player after the dealer leads the first trick. Everyone plays one card, going round. **Follow the suit led if you can.** If you can't, play anything.
- **Asking for the trump.** When you can't follow suit you may tap **ASK FOR TRUMP**. The trump is shown to everyone, the hidden card goes back into the bidder's hand, and you must play a trump if you have one. Until the trump is shown, trumps count as ordinary cards. The bidder may show it the same way.
- **Winning a trick.** The highest trump wins it (the trump counts in the trick where it is shown). With no trump in it, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads the next trick.

## The pair (a house rule)
The host can switch this off before the first game; it is on to begin with. Once the trump is shown, a player holding its **K and Q** whose side has already won a trick may tap **SHOW PAIR** on their turn. If they are on the bidding side, the bid drops by 4 (never under 16). If they are on the other side, the bid rises by 4 (never over 28). Only one pair a hand.

## Scoring
- After eight tricks, the bidding side counts the points in the tricks it took.
- **Made the bid:** the bidding side scores a game point. **Missed it:** the other side scores the game point.
- The first side to the target wins. The host picks **1, 3 or 6** hands; 3 to begin with.
- The host picks the rules before the first game only. **PLAY AGAIN** keeps them; at the end, **CHANGE** on the host's screen picks them for the next game. Playing alone against bots, the game starts straight away with the rules you last played.
- The deal moves on one seat every hand, and the first dealer moves on every game.

## Example
Asha bids 18 on J, 9 and A of hearts and the J of spades, and lays the 7 of hearts aside: hearts are trumps. Ben, on the other side, can't follow a spade lead and asks for the trump. Hearts are shown, and Ben, who holds a heart, has to play it. If Asha and her partner take 18 or more points between them, their side scores a game point.

## Controls
- Tap a number to bid it, or **PASS**.
- As the bidder, tap a card to lay it aside: its suit is the trump.
- Tap a card to play it. Cards you may play are raised and outlined.
- Tap **ASK FOR TRUMP** or **SHOW PAIR** when they appear. **NEXT HAND** moves on, or the table moves on by itself after a few seconds.
